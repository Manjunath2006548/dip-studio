Technique comparison

Global HE is compared with CLAHE and linear contrast stretching on the same four images.

Reading the comparison

Global HE gives the strongest, most uniform redistribution and lifts the shadowed evening and night images the most. CLAHE keeps noise in check by clipping each tile's histogram, so its contrast gain is smaller. Contrast stretching preserves the histogram shape and cannot recover detail that was never recorded.

Method

Why histogram equalization works, and what the pipeline does to each image.

1 · Illumination and intensity

A monochrome image is a two-dimensional array f(x, y) of M × N pixels with intensities 0 … L−1. The recorded value is the product of illumination and reflectance:

f(x, y) = i(x, y) · ro(x, y)

Weak light crowds intensities into the dark end of the scale; strong light clips them at the bright end. In both cases the usable dynamic range shrinks and the picture looks flat.

2 · Histogram, PDF and CDF

The histogram h(rk) = nk counts pixels per grey level. Dividing by M·N gives the PDF, and its running sum is the CDF:

p(rk) = nk / (M·N)   ·   c(rk) = Σj≤k p(rj)

3 · Equalization mapping

For continuous intensities the scaled CDF produces a uniform output PDF. For discrete 8-bit images the practical mapping subtracts the minimum non-zero CDF value:

sk = round[ (c(rk) − cmin) / (1 − cmin) · 255 ]

The mapping is monotonic, so grey-level order is preserved. Because levels are merged, the equalized histogram is only approximately flat — but its CDF becomes close to a straight line.

Pipeline used by this workbench

  1. Read the image, centre-crop to the working aspect ratio and resize with INTER_AREA.
  2. Convert to grayscale with ITU-R BT.601 weights: 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B.
  3. Compute the histogram, then the PDF and CDF with user-defined functions.
  4. Build the look-up table from Eq. (5) and apply it to every pixel.
  5. Cross-check against cv2.equalizeHist and produce CLAHE and percentile-stretch comparisons.
  6. Recompute histogram, PDF and CDF of the result and evaluate all quality parameters.

Simulating the four illuminations

The Day image is a real phone photograph. Midday, Evening and Night are produced in linear light (γ = 2.2) by applying an exposure gain — 2.6×, 0.20× and 0.012× respectively — a colour-temperature tint, graded sun glare for midday, a localised warm lamp pool for night, and signal-dependent sensor noise that grows as the scene darkens. The scene content and framing are identical in all four images; only the illumination changes.

Expected behaviour

HE should pull every condition's mean brightness close to mid-grey (≈128) and increase σ, with the largest gains for evening and night. Entropy may fall slightly because neighbouring grey levels are merged. In dark images the steep mapping also magnifies sensor noise, which is why CLAHE or denoising before equalization is preferable for noisy night scenes.
